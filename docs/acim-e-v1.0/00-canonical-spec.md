# 1. Executive definition

ACIM-E is a governed enterprise operating model for selecting, orienting, designing, simulating, piloting, validating, deploying, and continuously improving AI-enabled organizational workflows.

It is not a model-selection checklist, a generic digital-transformation roadmap, or a security-control catalog. It is an **implementation control system** binding organizational context, workflow evidence, authority, compliance, human outcomes, technical architecture, benchmarks, telemetry, and change governance into one lifecycle.

The canonical lifecycle is:

```text
Hailing Call
→ Gate 0 Orientation
→ Organization Knowledge Core
→ Golden Workflow Knowledge Baseline
→ Candidate Twin
→ Forecast
→ Reversible Pilot
→ Evaluated Deployment
→ Reconciliation
→ Gate 8 Controlled Commit
→ Continuous Telemetry and Benchmark Recalibration
```

The objective is not maximum automation. The objective is maximum validated organizational value within bounded authority, protected human outcomes, legal and compliance constraints, operational resilience, and reversible change.

ACIM-E integrates:

- Enterprise AI security and compliance orientation.
- The ACIM implementation lifecycle.
- WEOF gate-based workflow governance.
- Gate 0 loci, environment, authority, and reasoning-geometry controls.
- The Five-Track implementation model.
- Core, cross-cutting, relational, and meta-benchmark suites.
- UTSS scenario stress testing.
- Benchmark VOC and the Outcome Validation Score.
- Candidate Twins, forecast discipline, pilot reversibility, telemetry, and governed organizational learning.

## Normative doctrine

1. Orient before govern.
2. Baseline before optimize.
3. Simulate before irreversible action.
4. Pilot before scale.
5. Validate before trust.
6. Reconcile before commit.
7. Outcomes outrank internal scores.
8. Authority remains bounded.

# 2. Purpose, scope, and non-goals

## 2.1 Purpose

ACIM-E creates one controlled path from enterprise demand to production implementation. It is intended to:

- Prevent premature automation of poorly understood work.
- Separate organizational goals from model capabilities and vendor claims.
- Establish a defensible baseline before measuring improvement.
- Preserve human decision rights and escalation ownership.
- Bind implementation to jurisdiction, industry, organization type, business function, workflow, data class, and risk.
- Test proposed changes in simulation and bounded pilots.
- Evaluate performance through multiple benchmark layers.
- Detect Goodharting, benchmark gaming, hidden compensators, and second-order harm.
- Preserve provenance from request through organizational commit.
- Convert implementation evidence into reusable organizational knowledge.

## 2.2 Scope

ACIM-E applies to enterprise LLMs, retrieval systems, agentic and multi-agent systems, decision support, workflow automation, human-AI collaboration, and digital, physical, cyber-physical, or embodied systems capable of affecting organizational state.

It covers scientific, medical, educational, production, manufacturing, service, merchandising, administrative, and enterprise optimization workflows.

## 2.3 Non-goals

ACIM-E does not:

- Declare a system safe solely because it passes a static benchmark.
- Replace legal, regulatory, clinical, engineering, security, or domain review.
- Assume automation is the preferred end state.
- Treat employee or customer feedback as unrestricted data.
- Permit a child agent, workflow, or business unit to exceed parent authority.
- Convert speculative forecasts into production claims.
- Treat model self-reports as ground truth when independent observation exists.
- Guarantee ROI, compliance, safety, or reliability through documentation alone.

# 3. Core operating doctrine

## 3.1 Reality anchoring

Internal metrics are provisional. Operational reality, protected countermetrics, independent observation, and served-entity outcomes determine success.

## 3.2 Bounded authority

Every actor, agent, service, workflow, and child harness inherits parent constraints. Authority may be narrowed, conditioned, or escalated through an approved path; it may not be silently expanded.

## 3.3 Reversibility

Every material pilot defines rollback conditions, restoration procedures, decision ownership, evidence retention, and a maximum correction window.

## 3.4 Evidence separation

Facts, assumptions, hypotheses, forecasts, model outputs, operator observations, and approved organizational state remain separately typed.

## 3.5 Human outcome protection

Efficiency cannot override safety, rights, access, dignity, workload sustainability, fairness, privacy, or clinically appropriate care.

## 3.6 Multi-objective optimization

No high-impact workflow is optimized against one metric. Every primary objective requires at least one protected countermetric and one stop condition.

## 3.7 Provenance

Every promoted artifact preserves source identity, timestamp, transformation history, authority level, validation status, parent causal nodes, and commit state.

## 3.8 Fail-closed orientation

When orientation, authority, ownership, jurisdiction, evidence, or review is missing, work pauses. Ambiguity does not default to permission.

## 3.9 Adaptive reasoning geometry

Execution defaults to an internal tunnel. Discovery may use controlled exploration. Hybrid work may branch outward for candidate generation but must return through the internal tunnel for validation and promotion.

## 3.10 Continuous calibration

Benchmarks, policies, prompts, twins, and workflows are living controls recalibrated against outcomes and environmental change.

# 4. Integrated enterprise architecture

## 4.1 Recursive containment model

```text
Enterprise Governance Charter
└── ACIM-E Organizational Harness
    └── Gate 0 Orientation Container
        └── Implementation Track Harness
            └── Industry Grouping Overlay
                └── Regional and Jurisdictional Compliance Harness
                    └── Organization Harness
                        └── Business Model Cluster
                            └── Business Function Harness
                                └── Workflow Component Harness
                                    └── Agent / Process / Observer Triad
```

Containment rules:

- A child cannot exceed parent authority.
- A child inherits all parent constraints.
- A child may add constraints or request escalation.
- A child may not silently remove constraints.
- A child may not convert evidence into authority.

## 4.2 Control planes

**Governance plane:** authority, invariants, risk tolerance, escalation, review ownership, commit rights, and prohibited actions.

**Knowledge plane:** Organization Knowledge Core, Golden Workflow Knowledge Baseline, policies, data dictionaries, causal graphs, benchmark cases, provenance receipts, and approved memory.

**Execution plane:** models, agents, tools, integrations, human tasks, workflow components, and pilot operations within bounded permissions.

**Observation plane:** independent telemetry, exceptions, operator actions, human outcomes, model behavior, environmental changes, and discrepancies between self-report and observed behavior.

The execution plane cannot self-certify.

Every object has an owner, authority boundary, data classification, risk class, lifecycle state, and provenance reference.

# 5. Gate 0: Organizational Orientation Compiler

Gate 0 asks:

> What is being requested, who is acting, for whom, in which organizational and regulatory environment, against which workflow, with what authority, under which protected constraints, and through which permitted reasoning geometry?

Gate 0 converts an ambiguous enterprise request into a typed, bounded, jurisdiction-aware, authority-aware implementation vector.

## 5.1 Required inputs

- Goal trigger or enterprise demand.
- Organization and operating context.
- Sector and industry designation.
- Jurisdiction and operating regions.
- Organization type.
- Business model cluster.
- Business function.
- Workflow request and scope.
- Implementation track.
- VOC profile or declared absence.
- Human and compliance review ownership.
- Data classes.
- Allowed optimization targets.
- Forbidden targets and actions.
- Reversibility requirement.

## 5.2 Loci geometry

Loci establishes actor identity, role, current gate and workflow position, relationship to the organization and served entities, capability boundary, authority boundary, commit boundary, and escalation path.

A loci-preservation check prevents prompts, documents, tools, peer agents, vendor systems, or retrieved sources from repositioning an actor into an unauthorized identity or authority role.

## 5.3 Working-environment geometry

```text
Implementation track
→ sector
→ industry grouping
→ jurisdiction
→ regulated status
→ organization type
→ business model cluster
→ business function
→ workflow scope
→ human role
→ data classes
→ protected constraints
→ allowed optimization targets
→ forbidden targets
→ review ownership
```

No workflow enters execution without track, industry, regional, organizational, functional, workflow, authority, and data context.

## 5.4 Reasoning-expansion modes

**Internal tunnel:** production, compliance, security, legal, medical, financial, safety-critical, irreversible, or high-liability work. External content is candidate evidence, not control logic.

**Exploratory expansion:** scientific discovery, education, scenario generation, analogy search, design exploration, and hypothesis formation. Branches remain non-authoritative until validated.

**Hybrid:** external branching for discovery or option generation followed by mandatory return through the internal tunnel for evidence validation, authority checking, simulation, and promotion.

## 5.5 Mandatory controls

- Sealed external port.
- Admission contract.
- Invariant authorization seal.
- Provenance receipt.
- Loci-preservation check.
- Environment-boundary check.
- Expansion-mode selection.
- Hallucination correction path.
- Human exception node.
- Orientation expiry and refresh conditions.

External inputs cannot directly write authority, invariants, organizational identity, compliance posture, trusted state, or commit rights.

## 5.6 Outputs and stop conditions

A successful Gate 0 produces a Gate 0 Orientation Vector Packet and routes to target-workflow orientation.

Gate 0 stops when the requested outcome, organizational scope, jurisdiction, industry, authority, review ownership, data classification, or required oversight is missing; when optimization conflicts with protected countermetrics; when a source attempts to rewrite invariants; or when the action cannot be made reversible and no authorized exception exists.

The detailed subordinate Gate 0 specification is maintained in [`../gate-0-orientation-gate/`](../gate-0-orientation-gate/README.md).

# 6. Enterprise classification and context stack

ACIM-E uses parallel, nested dimensions:

- **Implementation track:** mission geometry of the AI implementation.
- **Sector and industry grouping:** economic, regulatory, and domain context.
- **Organization type:** private, public, government, nonprofit, research, education, healthcare, or hybrid institutional form.
- **Business model cluster:** manufacturing, service, merchandising, or hybrid value-flow overlay.
- **Business function:** operations, production, supply chain, finance, marketing, sales, customer service, HR, legal, compliance, cybersecurity, IT, research, education, clinical care, or another defined function.
- **Workflow:** bounded actors, decisions, information, tools, actions, handoffs, outcomes, and exceptions.

Compliance mapping occurs across jurisdictional law, sector regulation, data requirements, organizational policy, contracts, professional standards, security frameworks, and workflow-specific controls. The map must distinguish legal requirement, regulatory interpretation, contractual obligation, organizational policy, industry practice, technical standard, and recommended control.

# 7. ACIM-E implementation lifecycle

## Phase 0 — Hailing Call

Capture the enterprise demand without treating the proposed solution as validated. Record the sponsor, affected populations, claimed opportunity, known constraints, initial risk, reversibility screen, and whether to enter Gate 0.

“Deploy an LLM” is not an acceptable final problem definition.

## Phase 1 — Gate 0 Orientation

Compile organizational, authority, compliance, workflow, data, and reasoning context. Produce the Orientation Vector Packet, preliminary risk class, review owners, expansion mode, evidence plan, and proceed/stop/exception decision.

## Phase 2 — Organization Knowledge Core

The OKC contains the minimum sufficient enterprise context for the selected workflow:

- Mission, strategy, and operating model.
- Organization chart and decision rights.
- Business functions and accountable owners.
- Systems and data landscape.
- Policies, contracts, and controls.
- Roles, competencies, and handoffs.
- Failure modes and exceptions.
- Served-entity outcome definitions.
- Historical metrics and measurement defects.
- Change history and semantic controls.

## Phase 3 — Golden Workflow Knowledge Baseline

The Golden WFKB is the approved as-is workflow representation. It includes trigger, outcome, actors, authority, inputs, tools, decisions, handoffs, normal and exception paths, queues, cost, resources, failure modes, hidden compensators, controls, metrics, countermetrics, human outcomes, confidence, and data gaps.

It is “golden” because it is governed and evidence-linked—not because it is perfect.

## Phase 4 — Candidate Twin

A Candidate Twin is a simulation-addressable representation of a proposed workflow, organizational, technical, or authority change. It identifies changes, invariants, assumptions, dependencies, human and model roles, control points, benefits, risks, failure propagation, reversibility, evidence requirements, and evaluation plan.

## Phase 5 — Forecast

Forecasting reports plausible ranges rather than one deterministic result. It includes baseline comparison, best/expected/adverse cases, confidence bounds, sensitivity, direct and second-order effects, bottlenecks, workload, security, compliance, data-quality sensitivity, rollback thresholds, and measurement plan.

Forecasts cannot be promoted as achieved ROI or production evidence.

## Phase 6 — Reversible Pilot

A pilot defines population, scope, comparison method, time window, human review, allowed and blocked actions, rollback, incident response, telemetry, benchmark suite, VOC plan, decision thresholds, and owner.

The pilot is the smallest implementation capable of producing decision-quality evidence.

## Phase 7 — Evaluated Deployment

Advancement requires acceptable performance across core, cross-cutting, relational, security, compliance, operational, and human-outcome dimensions. Deployment remains conditionally authorized until stable across operating conditions, exceptions, data variation, user populations, and time.

## Phase 8 — Reconciliation and Controlled Commit

Reconciliation compares actual results against the Golden WFKB, approved constraints, forecast, telemetry, benchmark outcomes, VOC, incidents, and current organizational state.

The decision may approve full or limited integration, continue the pilot, require redesign, narrow authority, expand monitoring, roll back, or retire the implementation.

Only validated changes enter the OKC, WFKB, policy set, workflow state, benchmark library, or reusable implementation assets.

## Phase 9 — Continuous Operations

Production includes drift detection, benchmark recalibration, VOC, incident review, model and data change review, authority recertification, control testing, forecast-versus-actual analysis, and periodic return to Gate 0 when context materially changes.

# 8. WEOF governance and transition gates

- **Gate 0 — Organizational orientation:** loci, environment, authority, jurisdiction, track, workflow, data, constraints, reasoning mode.
- **Gate 1 — Target workflow orientation:** bounded workflow, affected entities, intended outcome, dependencies, review ownership.
- **Gate 2 — Baseline and evidence sufficiency:** OKC and Golden WFKB adequacy.
- **Gate 3 — Candidate design authorization:** assumptions, invariants, authority, risks, and criteria explicit.
- **Gate 4 — Forecast and simulation validation:** scenarios, stress tests, human outcomes, compliance, security, reversibility.
- **Gate 5 — Pilot authorization:** smallest bounded real-world test capable of decision-quality evidence.
- **Gate 6 — Operational evaluation:** evidence versus baseline, benchmarks, countermetrics, VOC, incidents, and forecast.
- **Gate 7 — Reconciliation:** contradictions, residual risk, ownership, commit recommendation.
- **Gate 8 — Organizational commit:** promotion into governed organizational state and memory.

Every gate produces a decision, evidence packet, authority record, exceptions, conditions, expiry or review date, and provenance receipt.

Execution agents do not own Gate 8 commit authority.

# 9. Five implementation tracks

## 9.1 AI Core Model of Implementation Optimization

The singular enabling track running through all others: orientation, governance, architecture, security, compliance, data, workflow design, model and agent selection, telemetry, evaluation, human oversight, and continuous improvement.

## 9.2 Production and Manufacturing

Production, manufacturing, logistics, maintenance, quality, supply chain, scheduling, industrial operations, and physical process control.

Protected countermetrics include safety, defect escape, rework, downtime, operator overload, unauthorized control, supply fragility, and quality degradation hidden by throughput.

## 9.3 Scientific Discovery

Defined, relational, novel, and unique scientific discovery.

Protected countermetrics include validation burden, reproducibility, evidentiary quality, interpretability, false discovery, methodological leakage, unsupported causal claims, and suppression of disconfirming evidence.

## 9.4 Medical

Patient care, treatment planning, clinical operations, documentation, research translation, and health-system workflows.

Protected countermetrics include false dismissal, delay, authority-boundary violation, inappropriate automation, equity, access, privacy, clinical workload, and loss of informed human review.

## 9.5 Organizational Optimization

Enterprise operations, service delivery, merchandising, finance, marketing, sales, customer service, HR, IT, governance, compliance, and management systems.

Protected countermetrics include repeat work, employee burden, customer effort, risk transfer, metric gaming, hidden manual compensation, quality loss, and decision-right erosion.

## 9.6 Education and Human Capability

Teaching, learning, training, skill formation, competency evaluation, curriculum, knowledge transfer, and human development.

Protected countermetrics include misconception introduction, dependency, skill atrophy, inappropriate difficulty, false confidence, loss of learner agency, accessibility failure, privacy, and evaluation invalidity.

# 10. Benchmark architecture

## 10.1 Hierarchy

### Five-Track Core Benchmarks

- **GPO-Bench** — production, manufacturing, and general operations.
- **SciDisc-Bench** — scientific discovery.
- **Medical-Bench** — medical and clinical implementation.
- **Optimization-Bench** — enterprise and organizational optimization.
- **Education-Bench** — education and human capability.

### Cross-Cutting Benchmarks

- TSC
- SecOps
- RevT
- RegComp
- OrgComm
- WTT

### Relational Benchmarks

- IPeer
- FedComp
- Maturity

### Meta-Benchmark

- CCIB

Canonical identifiers are preserved where long-form expansions have not yet been formally frozen. Each benchmark implementation maintains its definition, version, case library, scoring method, protected countermetrics, and validity evidence.

## 10.2 Minimum benchmark case structure

Every case defines track, domain, industry, jurisdiction, workflow, role, starting state, evidence, allowed tools, authority boundary, expected and forbidden behavior, primary metric, countermetrics, failure classes, human review, observability, scoring rubric, uncertainty, provenance, version, and retirement criteria.

## 10.3 UTSS scenario stress tests

- **Matrix Test:** hidden-state deception, conflicting control layers, and presented reality versus authorized truth.
- **Batdog Protocol:** multimodal or cross-domain reconstruction from fragmented signals without inventing authority.
- **Apollo 13 CO₂ Test:** resource-constrained emergency reasoning, prioritization, improvised design, communication, and safe operation.
- **Groundhog Day Test:** repeated-loop learning that incorporates prior failure evidence and measurably corrects strategy.

UTSS supplements, but does not replace, track benchmarks and production telemetry.

## 10.4 Anti-gaming requirements

Separate development and holdout cases, rotate cases, detect memorization, compare self-report to observation, test distribution shift, preserve failures, measure subgroups, and suspend optimization authority when benchmark scores improve while protected outcomes deteriorate.

# 11. Benchmark VOC and Outcome Validation Score

VOC is **governed served-entity signal intelligence**. It captures the experiences and outcomes of people or organizations affected by an AI-enabled workflow and uses that signal to detect fake optimization, reveal benchmark gaps, and recalibrate implementation.

VOC may include operator observations, customer effort, patient outcomes, learner performance, partner friction, exceptions, complaints, appeals, repeat work, downstream burden, and safety events.

## 11.1 Benchmark VOC cycle

```text
Stakeholder population
+ collection timeframe
+ benchmark score context
+ real outcome experience
+ identified gap
→ calibration signal
+ new benchmark cases
+ Outcome Validation Score
```

The central question is:

> Is the gap between benchmark score and operator- or served-entity-reported outcome experience narrowing over successive deployment cycles?

## 11.2 Outcome Validation Score

OVS evaluates the relationship between normalized benchmark-score change and normalized protected real-world outcome change across deployment cycles.

- **+1.0:** strong alignment.
- **0.0:** no stable relationship detected.
- **−1.0:** benchmark improvement is associated with outcome deterioration.

OVS is a calibration diagnostic, not causal proof.

## 11.3 Gap classes

- Calibration gap.
- Coverage gap.
- Case-library gap.
- Authority-boundary gap.
- Human-baseline gap.
- Unknown gap.

Repeated safety-critical gaps suspend benchmark-driven optimization authority pending recalibration.

## 11.4 Privacy and purpose limitation

VOC collection is purpose-limited, proportionate, transparent, access-controlled, retention-bounded, protected against retaliation, separated from unauthorized employee surveillance, and grounded in consent or another lawful basis where required.

# 12. Security, compliance, and data governance

## 12.1 Security architecture

Minimum domains include identity and access management, zero-trust principles, role- and attribute-based authorization, just-in-time permissions, secrets management, encryption, DLP, connector allowlisting, prompt-injection controls, model and supply-chain risk, independent logging, incident response, recovery, isolation, egress control, vulnerability management, and adversarial evaluation.

## 12.2 Data governance

Every data source has an owner, purpose, classification, lawful authority, quality status, freshness, retention rule, access rule, permitted model use, training-use status, geographic or contractual restriction, provenance, and deletion or correction path.

Baseline, simulation, pilot, and production data remain distinguishable.

## 12.3 Compliance integration

Compliance is integrated at orientation, design, pilot, deployment, and change. Evidence packets include applicable requirements, industry obligations, contractual controls, assurance mappings, policy, approvals, evidence plan, owners, test cadence, remediation, and audit trail.

Claims such as “compliant,” “secure,” “zero breach,” or “100 percent coverage” require defined scope, evidence, and accountable authority.

# 13. Human authority and organizational roles

ACIM-E separates recommendation, execution, exception, approval, commit, rollback, and audit authority.

Models and agents may hold recommendation or bounded execution authority. Material policy change, organizational commit, and irreversible high-impact action remain assigned to an explicitly authorized human role or governance body.

Required roles, proportionate to risk:

- Executive sponsor.
- Implementation owner.
- Workflow owner.
- Domain lead.
- Data owner or steward.
- Security owner.
- Compliance or legal owner.
- Human review owner.
- Operator representative.
- Served-entity or VOC owner.
- Independent observer or evaluation owner.
- Gate 8 commit authority.

Human-in-the-loop design verifies reviewer competence, realistic review time, sustainable escalation volume, measured review quality, alert fatigue, hidden cleanup work, and visible decision ownership.

# 14. Candidate Twin, forecasting, and simulation

## 14.1 Twin fidelity levels

- **A — Conceptual twin:** actors, decisions, information, constraints, causal assumptions.
- **B — Workflow twin:** sequence, queues, handoffs, timing, exceptions, resource behavior.
- **C — Data-connected shadow twin:** production-like data without production state change.
- **D — Bounded interactive twin:** controlled actions in sandbox or isolated pilot.
- **E — Production-linked operational twin:** monitored production decisions under explicit authority and rollback.

Higher fidelity requires stronger security, validation, observability, and governance.

## 14.2 Forecast discipline

Forecasts report assumptions, data coverage, confidence, sensitivity, unknowns, ranges, direct and indirect effects, distributional effects, manual compensation, time-to-learning, reversal cost, and residual risk.

## 14.3 Simulation suite

Simulation covers normal operations, peak load, data degradation, tool or model failure, prompt injection, conflicting instructions, authority escalation, stale state, domain drift, delayed human review, rare high-impact exceptions, rollback, recovery, and cross-workflow propagation.

# 15. Pilot, deployment, and scale

A strong pilot tests one bounded value hypothesis, preserves a baseline or comparator, instruments the workflow end to end, includes adverse cases, limits authority, has a rollback owner, measures workload, collects VOC, and produces reusable evidence.

Scale requires stable performance, acceptable subgroup results, controlled incidents, demonstrated rollback, sustainable review, security and compliance approval, positive or acceptable OVS trend, clear ownership, financial and operational viability, and updated organizational baselines.

Recommended authority progression:

1. Advisory only.
2. Human-reviewed recommendation.
3. Shadow execution.
4. Bounded execution with confirmation.
5. Exception-based supervision.
6. Conditional autonomy.
7. Expanded autonomy only after evidence, authority, and recertification.

Skipping levels requires documented justification and explicit risk acceptance.

---

# 16. Telemetry, monitoring, and continuous improvement

Telemetry covers inputs, data quality, model and agent outputs, tool use, authority checks, human review, overrides, exceptions, latency, availability, cost, security events, compliance evidence, workflow outcomes, protected countermetrics, VOC, forecast variance, benchmark drift, and environmental or policy change.

Independent observation detects disagreement between self-report and tool use, claimed completion and workflow state, benchmark score and human outcome, policy intent and behavior, forecast and actual effect, user instruction and authorized instruction, and local optimization and enterprise outcome.

Return to Gate 0 when jurisdiction, industry, regulated status, workflow scope, data class, authority, model, agent, vendor, tool, connector, affected population, benchmark, policy, ownership, or operating model materially changes—or when incidents or OVS reveal a context defect.

# 17. Measurement, value, and ROI discipline

Enterprise value is evaluated across time, cost, quality, revenue or mission impact, risk, resilience, workload, served-entity outcomes, compliance evidence, learning, capability formation, and strategic option value.

Value claims require a baseline period, comparison method, scope, data coverage, attribution method, full costs, displaced or transferred work, confidence, time horizon, and countermetrics.

Unsupported percentage claims from legacy enterprise materials are illustrative placeholders only. ACIM-E does not inherit unsupported guarantees.

The implementation dashboard reports gate status, baseline confidence, pilot status, primary outcomes, countermetrics, incidents, review volume and quality, cost and benefit, forecast variance, benchmarks, OVS, drift, open risks, and the next decision owner.

# 18. Organizational maturity model

- **Level 0 — Unoriented experimentation:** ad hoc AI use; unclear ownership, data use, authority, evaluation, and risk controls.
- **Level 1 — Inventoried:** use cases, models, vendors, data sources, and owners identified.
- **Level 2 — Bounded pilots:** Gate 0, baselines, review ownership, reversibility, and pilot controls used.
- **Level 3 — Governed implementation:** WEOF gates, benchmarks, security, compliance, telemetry, and VOC operate consistently.
- **Level 4 — Enterprise learning system:** twins, reusable baselines, recalibration, cross-workflow learning, and Gate 8 discipline institutionalized.
- **Level 5 — Adaptive governed enterprise:** dynamic reorientation, simulation, piloting, scaling, authority narrowing, and cross-system learning while preserving human outcomes and enterprise control.

Maturity never waives risk controls.

# 19. Stop conditions and failure handling

Mandatory pause or rollback conditions include unauthorized authority expansion, missing ownership, unresolved high-severity security or compliance issues, uncertain data rights, protected-countermetric deterioration, benchmark-outcome divergence, unobservable execution, failed restoration, behavior outside the validated envelope, reviewer overload, hidden manual compensation, subgroup harm, stale orientation, and provenance loss.

Failure handling sequence:

```text
Detect
→ contain
→ preserve evidence
→ notify owner
→ narrow or suspend authority
→ assess impact
→ restore safe state
→ analyze cause
→ recalibrate controls and cases
→ reauthorize through the appropriate gate
```

# 23. Source annotations

## A1 — Legacy enterprise metrics

The January 2025 Enterprise LLM Implementation document contains percentage improvements, uptime, threat-prevention, audit-success, breach, and ROI claims without deployment evidence, sample definitions, calculation methods, or confidence bounds. ACIM-E preserves the useful categories but replaces unsupported guarantees with evidence protocols.

## A2 — Gate 0 evolution

Gate 0 evolved from WEOF self-orientation into an organizational orientation compiler and Cognitive ETL boundary, adding loci preservation, silo geometry, sealed external admission, authority invariants, provenance receipts, and adaptive reasoning geometry.

## A3 — Industry grouping

Five Tracks, industry classification, business model cluster, business function, and workflow are separate but nested dimensions. Manufacturing, service, and merchandising are operating clusters—not substitutes for sector and industry classification.

## A4 — Benchmark identifiers

Core benchmark names are stable. Several cross-cutting, relational, and meta identifiers remain canonical acronyms whose long-form names have not been frozen. This specification does not invent expansions.

## A5 — Outcome Validation Score

OVS measures score-outcome alignment over cycles, not causation. Sample counts, shifting populations, delayed effects, confounding changes, and measurement quality must be reported.

## A6 — Synthetic Gate 0 sandbox

Gate 0 sandbox work tested structural logic against injection, authority escalation, stale state, domain drift, discovery, and time-constrained scenarios. It was not live-model certification.

## A7 — WEOF gate profile

Section 8 is the integrated ACIM-E organizational gate profile. It preserves orientation, validation, reconciliation, and Gate 8 commit doctrine while forming a complete enterprise sequence.

## A8 — Regulatory interpretation

The compliance sections provide implementation structure, not legal advice. Requirements must be validated for the actual organization, jurisdiction, industry, data, and workflow.

## A9 — Human baseline

Human performance is not presumed optimal or unbiased. It is a comparator representing the current workflow; both human and AI-enabled baselines are evaluated against protected outcomes.

## A10 — Model independence

ACIM-E is model-, vendor-, and deployment-pattern-independent. Substitutions require controlled change, revalidation, and reorientation when the risk surface changes.
